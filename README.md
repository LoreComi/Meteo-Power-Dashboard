pre-aggregated sandbox tables, plus a Databricks notebook that refreshes those tables
on a Lakeflow schedule. Nothing here runs locally — deploy both pieces to the workspace.

### Forecast → Weather Regimes

Port of `Franziska_Intern/corso_model_wr/working_wr_tool` (`uber_main.py` → `run_WR_tool.py` → `max_proj_members.py`), reading Databricks instead of the DSL NetCDF drops in `gph_frcst/`.

**Method.** Per member and per forecast day: the 500 hPa height anomaly over the fixed domain (lat 30–90 N, lon 80 W–40 E, 0.5° = 121 × 241) is divided by that day-of-year's domain-average amplitude, projected onto the 7 fixed regime patterns with cos(latitude) weighting, and standardised into an index (IWR) against each regime's own long-term mean and spread. The member takes the highest-scoring regime if it clears `1.00 + lead × −0.018774` (1.00 at day 0 → 0.74 at day 14; the slope is refitted from `corso_model_wr/wr_lead/WR_15_day_running_mean_leadtime_*`), otherwise "no regime". Isolated one-day flips are relabelled "no regime", as `clean_singles_2` does.

**Why the projection lives in the notebook.** It is an area-weighted inner product, so it is a join on `(latitude, longitude)` plus a group-by `(member, day)`: the ~22 M gridded values of a run stay in Spark and only ~900 rows per run are written. The app reads those.

**Differences from the tool, and why:**

| | Tool | Here |
|---|---|---|
| Source | DSL download → NetCDF on P: | `dna_prod_silver.meteomatics.geopotential_height_forecast` |
| Ensemble | ECMWF IFS-ENS, 51 members | **ECMWF AIFS-ENS, 50** (default) and **NCEP GFS-ENS, 30** — the silver layer has per-member Z500 only for these two, so shares are out of 50 or 30, not 51 |
| Anomalies | `gph_climo_domain_05_inst_common.mat` | ERA5 day-of-year mean computed in the notebook (`wr_gph_clim`) |
| Horizon | 15 days | 15 by default (the threshold's decay was fitted over leads 0–14); up to 17 available, with the extrapolation flagged |
| Climatology | `WR_reanalysis_daily_1979_2024.csv` | `wr_reanalysis_daily` — ERA5 classified through the same projection, backfilled once then appended daily |
| Not ported | member scoring (12 `general_scoring_*.xlsx`), k-means scenario clustering, Telegram delivery | — |

**One file must be uploaded**, because it cannot be derived from Databricks: `weather-power-desk-app/wr_patterns.npz` (1.5 MB) holds the 7 fixed patterns, their normalisation constants and the 366-entry day-of-year amplitude — the frozen output of the original 1979–2019 k-means study, extracted losslessly from `weather_regimes_mimic_CMG/LCD_1979_2019_inst.nc`. Put it anywhere the notebook can read (Workspace path or UC volume) and point `WR_PATTERNS_PATH` at it; it is read once, on the first run, into `wr_patterns` / `wr_norm` / `wr_amplitude`.

**Verification.** The projection was checked against the tool's own `wr_proj()` on a real run (`20260921_00`): identical to 1e-9 across all 51 × 15 × 7 values, and 1118× faster. The shipped patterns reproduce the tool's classified reanalysis exactly — IWR to <1e-4 and 100 % regime agreement on 300 sampled days. Rebuilding anomalies from absolute heights plus a day-of-year climatology (what the notebook does, instead of the original `.mat`) also gives 100 % regime agreement, which is what makes deriving the climatology from ERA5 safe. The generated SQL was executed on DuckDB against 350 k synthetic grid rows and reproduces the array projection exactly, including that a misaligned grid yields an empty join rather than a silent partial sum.

### Morning Call — the two agent families

The free-text boxes are gone; `_ai_brief.py` runs two families over the numbers already on screen (and nothing else — no web, no news):

| Family | Specialists | Synthesis |
|---|---|---|
| **Power market** | temperature → load (France's electric heating, summer cooling and thermal derates, Nordic snowmelt) · wind & solar → residual load and the merit order (spreads, the midday block, negative-price setups) · precipitation → hydro by catchment (Alps, Nordic, SEE, Iberia) and over what horizon it bites | nets the three, names where they reinforce or offset, the part of the curve it applies to, and what would flip it |
| **Gas market** | temperature → LDZ heating demand (the non-linear heating range, thermal inertia) · wind & solar → gas-for-power displacement | nets the two demand legs, quoting the net in GWh |

Each specialist is shown only its own rows, plus the run pairing, the date, the season and — when the run covers a window only partly — the day count, so a four-day mean is never read as a full week. Each ends on a `SIGNAL: BULLISH|BEARISH|NEUTRAL` line, stripped from the prose and rendered as a card; the synthesis card is the family's net read. Two expanders show the specialist reads and the exact document each agent was given.

The gas family is additionally handed the Gas Demand section's LDZ and displaced-gas figures for the same runs (`_gas.gas_demand_snapshot`, computed on demand whether or not the section has been opened), with their sign conventions spelled out, so the commentary and the GWh cannot drift apart. Without Azure OpenAI credentials the table renders in full and only the commentary is unavailable.

## Method notes

- **Population-weighted country means (Meteomatics)**: a country's temperature is what its people feel, so the gridded Meteomatics fields are reduced to countries with GHS-POP 2025 weights rather than an unweighted average over a lat/lon box. `build_pop_weights.py` assigns population to countries at the raster's own ~1 km resolution (country polygon rasterised onto the 30″ grid) and only then sums it into the 0.5° cells — a 60 × 60 block sum, exact — so a border cell carries both countries' shares and coastal population is kept even where the 0.5° cell centre is at sea, the two things a mask drawn at 0.5° gets wrong. Persons, not fractions, are stored: a group (Nordics, Iberia, SEE) is then weighted right by summing over its countries, and Hungary can sit in both `hu` and `see` because the region mapping is a join, not a CASE. In the notebook the mean is `SUM(value × population) / SUM(population)` after joining the weights on the coordinates snapped to the 0.5° grid (`ROUND(x × 2) / 2`, so float representation can never empty the join), and cell 1a reports what share of the population actually met a forecast point. Country totals from the weights are within a few percent of Eurostat; Spain, Portugal and France are a little low because the Canaries, Madeira / Azores and the overseas departments lie outside the European window on purpose. Verified on DuckDB against a synthetic 0.5° field: the SQL reproduces the pandas weighted means to 1e-12 for all 21 countries and 8 regions.
- **Spread vs normal spread**: for each EC-ENS 00z run in the last 365 days, the std across members of the daily country mean, per lead day; the app compares today's std with the mean (and P25–P75) for the same lead day, preferring the same calendar month when ≥10 runs exist. Ratio ≥1.3 flags high uncertainty, ≤0.7 unusual confidence.
- **Scenarios**: features = weekly-mean temperature anomaly per (country × ISO week), weeks needing ≥4 forecast days; standardised; k-means (n_init=20) with k fixed at 2 (user can raise to 4); clusters relabelled by size (Scenario 1 = consensus); clusters <3 members folded into "Other". Member IDs are normalised (`M07`, `07`, `ens07` → 7) so a cluster found on one provider is read on another — all providers are the same 50 ECMWF perturbations. Silhouette is shown only as a separability diagnostic.
- **Morning Call**: `morning_daily` holds, per run of the last 8 days and per exact wapi curve name, the daily CET 'Avg' mean (tt/wnd/spv) or daily sum (rre) plus the normal. The app drops partial days (<90 % of the 15-min points, the report's `[:-1]`) and reproduces the weekly-window means and the precipitation sums exactly as the script does. Runs are keyed on their init time (not the init date), so a model with 00z and 12z cycles never has the two merged. Every column is paired with *its own* run `prev_days` earlier under the reference's weekday rule (−24h; −72h on a Monday) — or under an explicit rule the user picks — and when that run is missing from the table the nearest earlier one is used and the footer says so. Precipitation is summed over the first 15 forecast days of each run (EC-ENS's whole range), so GFS (16 days) and EC-Extended (46) are compared like for like.
- **Hydro**: `_hydro_quantify.build_climatology` / `quantify_anomaly` replicate the report scripts line by line (leap day dropped, dummy-year pivot, Volue normal from the last full year, percentile of this week's mean against the same week in history; hydro balance uses mean-of-years as norm).
- **Gas Demand**: `gas_demand_daily` holds, per pattern and per run of the last 8 days, the daily CET mean of the `'Avg'` tag for temperature (°C), wind and solar (GW, i.e. MWh/h × 0.001) plus the normal, for DE UK FR BE NL IT ES PT. `fcst_daily` cannot serve this — it keeps only six runs *per model family* and the EC-ENS family interleaves 00z and 12z, so a Monday's "ec00ens vs the ec00ens of three days ago" falls outside its window. The run pairing and the delta window are the scripts' exactly: a 00z run against the same pattern's previous 00z (three days back on a Monday), a 12z run against the same `date_min`'s 00z (two days back on a Monday); the window opens the day after `date_min` and closes 13 days after it, or on `date_min` + 11 days on a Monday — which is in both cases the last day of the older run's 15-day horizon, hence the shorter Monday window. Deltas are summed inclusively over that window. LDZ is bullish-positive, displaced gas bearish-positive; ±1 000 GWh is the cumulative trade threshold. Trailing actual temperature comes from `hist_daily` (`'AF'`), which is the quantity `ldz_forecast.py` was approximating with a 1-day-ahead deterministic forecast; the combined actual-plus-forecast path is forced gap-free before smoothing, because `smooth_temperature` shifts by position and a missing day would silently misalign the weights. Iberia is ES + PT (the scripts use Volue's `ib` aggregate, which covers the same two grids); a region missing a grid is summed from what is present and flagged, since that shifts the level but not the run-over-run delta.
- **Morning Call commentary**: seven LLM calls at most (power 3 + 1, gas 2 + 1), temperature 0.3, one independent call per specialist so no specialist can anchor another, and a synthesis that sees only the specialists' prose plus — for gas — the desk's own LDZ and displaced-gas figures. Week 3 reaches the agents only when the run covers at least `SCENARIO_MIN_WEEK_DAYS` of it, the same minimum the scenario engine applies to a forecast week.
